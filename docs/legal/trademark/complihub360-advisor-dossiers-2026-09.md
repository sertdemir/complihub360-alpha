This is a factual description of what the CompliHub360 platform does, written so that the three class dossiers can each be read on their own. Sections 2, 3 and 4 do not depend on one another and may be routed separately.

Everything here was established by going through the product itself, function by function, rather than by restating the draft identifications. Where the product does not support a phrase in the current draft wording, this document says so. Where the product does something the draft wording does not reach, it says that too.

Every function carries a status: **Implemented** (live in the product today), **In development** (built but not yet complete or not yet released), or **Planned** (a genuine, specified intention that is not yet built). Nothing is listed that does not exist or is not genuinely intended.

# 1 · Summary

## What CompliHub360 is

A technology platform that connects businesses trading across borders with independent professional service providers, and that publishes the regulatory information those businesses need in order to know what kind of help they require.

The sequence is the product:

1. A company answers a short guided assessment — which markets it sells into, how it operates, which areas concern it.
2. A rules engine converts those answers into a list of the regulatory obligations that plausibly apply to it, each with the statute behind it, a severity, a penalty range and a deadline.
3. The same engine scores a curated population of vetted independent specialist firms against that profile and returns a ranked shortlist, presented anonymously.
4. The company books an appointment. At that moment the specialist's identity is released, the company's dossier goes to the specialist, and **the platform's role ends.**

CompliHub360 performs **no** regulated professional service. It does not prepare or file tax returns, register producer-responsibility schemes, clear customs, draft contracts, act as a data protection officer, or certify products. Independent providers do that work, under their own contracts with the client.

The platform covers **nine regulatory areas** across **eight national markets** (Germany, France, United Kingdom, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Türkiye and the **United States**), in four interface languages.

## The three layers

| Class | Layer | What it protects |
|---|---|---|
| **35** | Intermediary | Identifying which professional service a business needs; provider discovery, comparison, referral and matching; administration of both sides of the marketplace |

## The FDA position

The applicant's product was searched for references to the Food and Drug Administration. **Two were found, and both have been removed** (17 September 2026):

- a citation in the obligation data, where a U.S. health-claims duty was sourced to "FTC Act §5 / FDA labeling rules". It now reads **"FTC Act §5 + FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance"** — the Commission's own 2022 guidance on applying the §5 standard to health claims. FTC authority throughout, no FDA component.
- an advertised scope claim in the marketing copy, where the market page listed **"FDA/Labeling"** as a United States focus area in all four languages. It now reads **"FTC Advertising Rules"**.

The second was the more exposed of the two, and counsel may wish to note the distinction: the first was a citation inside a data record — a statement about *what the law is*. The second was an advertised statement about *what the service covers in the United States*, which is precisely what a services identification and its exclusion govern.

A full-text search of the shipped source and copy now returns no occurrence of "FDA".

**The exclusion must not be read more broadly than the regulator.** The platform's product-compliance area is EU and UK product safety and CE conformity — the General Product Safety Regulation, CE marking, technical documentation, labelling — enforced by national market surveillance authorities, not by the FDA. Packaging and producer-responsibility duties rest on EU and national packaging law. All of this is genuine non-FDA product-regulatory activity and should survive the exclusion intact.

## Points counsel may wish to settle

1. **"Business contacts"** in the Class 35 draft appears unsupported. Provider contact details are deliberately withheld until an appointment is booked; the searchable database is one of business capabilities and attributes.
2. **Appointment scheduling** is the conversion event and the billing event of the entire business model, and is not reached by any of the three draft identifications.
3. **Provider vetting and verification** — the platform's central trust claim — appears in no class.
4. Two fields, **environmental compliance** and **marketing**, were confirmed by the founder as genuinely intended in their broad sense. Both have since been built out (18 September 2026); see the notes in sections 2 and 3 for what is in use and what remains intention.

# 2 · TRADEMARK ADVISOR DOSSIER — CLASS 35

*Business information, professional-needs identification, referral and provider matching*

## 2.1 Purpose of this class

Class 35 protects the intermediary business: taking business information from a company, identifying which categories of independent professional assistance it needs, maintaining and presenting a curated searchable population of vetted providers, ranking and comparing them, arranging the appointment that constitutes the referral, and administering the relationship on both sides of the marketplace.

## 2.2 What CompliHub360 does

**Identifying the need — Implemented.** A company completes a four-step guided assessment: the markets it sells into, how it operates (direct-to-consumer, business-to-business wholesale, marketplace, software subscription, hybrid), and the areas that concern it. Alternatively it types a question in plain language. From those answers the platform produces a named list of professional-service needs — for example "VAT registration and filing in Germany and the Netherlands", "packaging producer registration in Germany", "consent records under EU and German privacy law" — each marked as either confirmed (an area the company selected) or likely (an area the engine identified on its behalf).

**Curating the provider population — Implemented; vetting is deliberately a human step.** Firms are recruited business-to-business, or apply through a public application form. A recruited firm then submits its full package through a token-gated intake link: company details, certifications, specialisations, countries and languages served, billing model, pricing table, and a confirmation of the platform's standards. The submitted VAT identification number is validated automatically against the EU VIES service and the result is stored with its timestamp. **A firm becomes visible in any listing only after a human review.** A firm can apply; it cannot list itself.

**Ranking and comparison — Implemented.** Providers whose recorded coverage includes the company's country are scored on three components: relevance (country coverage and how many of the company's requested areas the firm actually covers), measured service quality (client rating, confirmation rate, responsiveness, and any service-level breaches), and partner status. Firms that are out of office, or that have been downgraded for missed response commitments, are ranked down automatically. The company receives a ranked shortlist together with a breakdown of what each match percentage rests on — which country matched, and which of its own requested areas the firm covers and which it does not. **The ranking is deterministic and is not for sale; the platform states this publicly.**

**Anonymity as a feature of the referral — Implemented.** Providers are presented by pseudonym and attribute, not by name: region, years in practice, specialisations, languages, rating, number of completed mandates, average response time, billing model, and a verification badge. A company may open a fuller profile, including the complete pricing table, while the firm is still anonymous. Name, logo, website and contact details are released only when an appointment is booked.

**The referral itself — Implemented.** The company selects an available slot and confirms. At that moment the provider's identity and contact details are released to the company, the company's dossier is released to the provider, and the platform's task is complete.

**Holding the referral to its promise — Implemented.** An automated watchdog reminds a provider whose confirmation deadline is approaching, records a breach when it passes, downgrades a firm's visibility once breaches accumulate, expires stale requests, and requests reviews from both sides afterwards. The platform commits publicly to a first response within 24 to 48 hours, failing which it routes the matter to the next available partner.

**Reviews — Implemented.** Reviews are accepted only from firms and clients who actually had a booking. Client ratings feed back into the quality component of the ranking.

**Services to providers — Implemented.** Providers receive a workspace for their bookings and performance indicators, transparency about their current ranking and what moves it, controls for the markets, languages, areas and service-level targets they cover, and a monthly invoice for the platform's own fees.

## 2.3 What independent providers do

All of the specialist work: tax and VAT registrations and filings, producer-responsibility scheme registration and reporting, customs declarations and classification, privacy assessments and data protection officer services, product conformity assessment and certification, contract drafting and legal opinions, corporate formation and secretarial filings, advertising clearance, and freight and logistics services.

## 2.4 Fields in which the referral operates

Tax and value-added tax · packaging and producer responsibility · environmental compliance · data privacy and data protection · advertising and marketing compliance, and marketing services · corporate formation, structuring, beneficial-ownership and know-your-business duties · product regulatory compliance and product safety (non-FDA) · customs · logistics · legal advisory.

## 2.5 Limitations

The FDA exclusion is retained. CompliHub360 performs no regulated professional service. Provider contact details are withheld by design until an appointment is booked.

## 2.6 What the current draft wording may not reach

- **Arranging and scheduling appointments between businesses and providers.** This is both the moment the referral happens and the moment the platform earns its fee. Nothing in the current wording reaches it.
- **Vetting and verification of providers**, and the resulting "Verified Partner" designation — the platform's central trust claim.
- **Collecting and publishing verified reviews and ratings** of providers, and using them to rank.
- **Business administration and invoicing services rendered to the providers.**
- **The staged-disclosure model**, which distinguishes this referral service from a directory.
- **Know-your-business, beneficial ownership and anti-money-laundering** as a field. It is present in the product and may or may not be read into "corporate formation, structuring and compliance".

**Possibly unsupported as drafted:** "an online searchable database featuring business information and **business contacts**". Contact data is not a browsable field of this database; it is the outcome of a completed referral.

## 2.7 Questions for counsel

Should the identification cover the provider-facing side of the marketplace at all? And does provider credential verification belong in this class or in Class 45?

# 3 · TRADEMARK ADVISOR DOSSIER — CLASS 42

*Software as a service, and the technology platform*

## 3.1 Purpose of this class

Class 42 protects the software: a hosted, multi-tenant web application that collects structured business information, applies a deterministic rules engine to identify and organise regulatory obligations, resolves jurisdiction-specific values, powers a scored provider search with server-enforced staged anonymisation, and delivers scheduling, reporting, document-handling and workspace functions to both sides of the marketplace.

**This is the class where precision matters most**, given the cited marks. The distinguishing facts are set out at 3.5.

## 3.2 What the software does

**Collecting structured information — Implemented.** A guided four-step interface produces a typed business profile: country, markets, operating model, areas of concern, risk signals and revenue band. Deep links are honoured, so a company arriving from a country page is not asked again which country it operates in.

**The obligation engine — Implemented.** This is the most technically specific part of the product and is worth describing precisely.

The engine holds, for each of eight markets, a weighting from one to ten for each regulatory area, together with enforcement-intensity and strictness scores. It holds a library of obligation templates across nine areas. And it holds, for each obligation and each country, the governing statute, a penalty range, a filing cadence, a typical lead time, and — where an act is adopted but not yet applicable — the date from which it begins to bite.

Given a business profile, the engine aggregates the country weightings across the requested markets, applies industry and operating-model modifiers, ranks and selects the areas in scope, expands them into individual obligations, filters by operating model without ever discarding an area the company explicitly asked about, enriches each obligation with its legal metadata, attaches the official identifier of the underlying EU act together with a deep link to the authoritative text, derives a severity band, and returns a sorted, citable set.

**The engine is rules-based and deterministic. It is not machine learning.** Its output is reproducible and every line traces to a named statute. This is a meaningful distinction from generic compliance software and counsel may wish to use it.

**Jurisdiction resolution — Implemented.** Reference values are held at country, region and locality level, and the engine returns the value from the most specific matching level. Where a user's region is unknown, only country-level values apply — deliberately, so that a user is never shown two contradictory state-level figures presented as equally authoritative. This exists because there is no nationwide sales-tax rate or nexus threshold in the United States; without it the product could not say anything correct about that market.

**Source verification — Implemented.** The platform's tax figures are reconciled weekly against the official EU tax database, which the member states populate themselves and which is openly licensed. The job fails loudly when a member state changes a rate the product still carries differently. **It writes nothing by design**: a tax figure that corrects itself without human review has no place in a compliance product. The run reports, a person decides, and the correction is made deliberately.

**Provider search and matching — Implemented.** The software filters providers by recorded coverage, reconciles two vocabularies of area names, scores relevance, measured quality and partner status, and ranks the result.

**Staged disclosure — Implemented.** The three-stage anonymity model is enforced by the server at the point of reading, not by the interface. Attributes and pricing are served while identity is withheld; identity is released only once a booking exists.

**Workspaces — Implemented.** Accounts, with guest sessions adopted into an account on registration so that a profile survives the sign-up step. Role-separated workspaces for companies, providers and administrators. Saved assessments that can be renamed, duplicated and archived. Saved providers. A per-area view that reads one area across all of a company's saved assessments.

**Recording progress — Implemented.** A company can record its own handling state against each identified obligation: open, in progress, done, or not applicable. The software keeps two axes strictly apart — **whether a duty applies is the system's judgment; whether it has been handled is the user's statement.** The platform stores what the user says; it does not verify, confirm or certify that anything was in fact done. See 3.5.

**Reporting — Implemented.** The platform generates a portable report of a company's obligations, with every legal reference resolved and listed with its official origin, and with a strict rule in code about which facts may appear in it: markets, areas and operating model, never notes, names or e-mail addresses.

**Scheduling — Implemented; live calendar synchronisation Planned.** Slot presentation, booking, prevention of double-booking, rescheduling with automatic notification, and outcome states. A native scheduler exists rather than an external calendar link because an external link would disclose the provider's identity and break the anonymity model. Synchronisation with providers' own calendars is specified and the vendor selected.

**Document handling — Implemented.** Uploaded documents pass through an automated redaction step **before any storage**; only the sanitised content is retained. Automated processing of a document requires a separate, explicit consent, which is re-checked at the moment processing is requested so that a withdrawal takes effect immediately.

**Notifications — Implemented; the user-facing preference screen is In development.** A per-role event feed with read state, filters and deep links; transactional e-mail with single-use action links. The preference model defines six alert types and is wired to a live endpoint.

**Provider-side software — Implemented.** Coverage and availability controls, service-level targets, performance indicators, ranking transparency, and invoice management.

**AI-assisted answering — Planned (intent-to-use).** A retrieval-augmented assistant over a curated internal knowledge base is built but is switched off behind a feature flag and has not been released. **What ships today is deterministic.** The applicant intends to claim this narrowly, by the action it performs — retrieving and summarising regulatory information from a curated knowledge base — rather than as "AI-powered compliance software", on the reasoning at 3.5.

## 3.3 Does the software perform the professional service?

No. It identifies and organises needs, finds and ranks the firms qualified to meet them, and administers the meeting. Where a user records that an obligation has been handled, the platform stores that statement as the user's own.

## 3.4 Note on two fields confirmed by the founder

**Environmental compliance.** Until 18 September 2026 the product carried environmental duties only as packaging law. It now has a standalone environmental area with three duties: producer registration for electrical and electronic equipment, producer responsibility for batteries, and the information duty for substances of very high concern in goods. Each rests on a named EU act with its official identifier. Areas that remain intention rather than use: emissions, chemicals beyond the substance-information duty, waste streams other than packaging and equipment, and environmental permitting.

**Marketing.** The field covers advertising-law and consumer-protection specialists, which has been in use throughout, **and** marketing service providers — agencies, search and paid media — for which the matching layer was extended on 18 September 2026. Whether firms in those categories have been onboarded is a supply question separate from the software's capability.

## 3.5 Distance from the cited marks, and language to avoid

The cited marks occupy generic compliance-management and compliance-monitoring software. CompliHub360's centre of gravity is different, and the following facts are verifiable in the product:

- the engine's output is **a needs list that terminates in a referral**, not a compliance workflow;
- there is **no remediation, task assignment, evidence collection, control testing or audit preparation** functionality anywhere in the product;
- the platform's declared boundary ends at the booked appointment;
- provider search, scoring, anonymisation and scheduling have no counterpart in compliance-management software;
- where the product does touch progress, it records the user's own statement and is architecturally separated from the system's own judgment about applicability.

**Language the applicant suggests avoiding:** "compliance management", "compliance monitoring", "compliance tracking", "regulatory change management", "compliance platform", "GRC".

**Language that creates the distance:** the matching clause. The applicant's view is that it should stay prominent rather than trailing at the end of the identification.

**One function deserves deliberate wording.** The obligation handling states described at 3.2 are the product's closest point of contact with the cited marks. The applicant suggests that any wording carry the distinction the product itself draws — software enabling users to record their own handling status against obligations previously identified for them — rather than anything resembling compliance tracking.

## 3.6 What the current draft wording may not reach

Scheduling software · report generation and export · automated redaction of personal data in documents, and consent-gated processing · saved searches, saved providers and workspaces · recording of handling states · provider-side software · the staged-disclosure engine · notification and alerting infrastructure · review capture and its feedback into ranking · jurisdiction-level data resolution · source verification against official databases · audit logging · the AI assistant.

## 3.7 Questions for counsel

How far should the identification go in naming the handling-state function, given its proximity to the cited marks? And should the document-redaction pipeline be named explicitly, as a distinctive function that sits well away from the crowded field?

# 4 · TRADEMARK ADVISOR DOSSIER — CLASS 45

*Regulatory compliance information provided by CompliHub360*

## 4.1 Purpose of this class

Class 45 protects the regulatory information CompliHub360 publishes in its own voice: what the law requires in a given market and area, which statute it rests on, what the exposure is, when it must be done, and when an adopted act begins to apply.

## 4.2 The discipline behind the content

Nothing on the informational surfaces is authored marketing prose. Every duty shown is generated from a verified body of legal metadata that was checked against the national texts and the EU Official Journal when it was written. Where the engine holds no national source, the page says so and shows the EU-level instrument labelled as such, rather than presenting a placeholder as a citation. Where coverage is thin, the page states how thin.

Two consequences are worth counsel's attention, because they bear on whether this is information or advice. First, the platform publishes **less** than it could, and says where it stops. Second, in September 2026 the applicant removed a body of content from an earlier version of the site — customer stories and guides that did not correspond to anything real — on the stated principle that a fabricated reference is worse than an empty page.

## 4.3 What CompliHub360 publishes

**Nine area pages — Implemented.** For each regulatory area, switchable by market: every duty the engine carries with its named statute; a note stating how many duties have a source specific to that market as against an EU regulation that applies directly; who enforces and what the stated penalty ranges are, with upper bounds labelled as upper bounds and not as expected cost; a timeline separating what applies now from what is settled law but not yet applicable; how the eight markets weight the area differently; and the related areas that share triggers with it.

**Eight market pages — Implemented.** The same body of data organised by country, naming the national statute in each case — the German, British, French, Italian, Spanish, Dutch and Turkish acts respectively, and for the United States the state-level economic-nexus rules and the federal corporate tax provisions.

**The generated obligation report — Implemented.** The same corpus filtered to one company's markets, operating model and product. Free, complete, and available in part without an account.

**A portable report — Implemented.** The same content as a document the company can keep, with every legal reference resolved to its official origin.

**A per-area workspace view — Implemented.** One regulatory area read across all of a company's saved assessments.

**Explanatory pages — Implemented.** How obligation identification works, what each area covers and whom it affects, what the platform can and cannot tell a company, and what happens to the company's data.

**Source provenance — Implemented.** Since September 2026 the platform's tax figures reconcile to an official, openly licensed EU database maintained by the member states themselves, with a weekly check for divergence. The information the platform publishes is therefore not merely asserted; it is traceable to a source the company can be shown.

**A knowledge library — In development.** The interface is built. There is no content pipeline behind it yet, and the applicant does not describe it as live.

**Regulatory-change alerts — Planned (intent-to-use).** Notification to a registered company when an obligation relevant to its saved assessment changes, or when a deadline approaches. The feature is announced on the site, specified to six alert types, and its preference model is wired to a live endpoint; detection and delivery do not yet exist. The applicant has corrected marketing copy that previously described this feature in the present tense.

## 4.4 Fields

Taxation and value-added tax · packaging and producer-responsibility environmental law · equipment, battery and substance duties · data privacy and data protection · advertising and marketing law, including health and sustainability claims · corporate formation, commercial register, beneficial ownership and know-your-business duties · product safety and product regulatory compliance (non-FDA) · customs · transportation and logistics · consumer protection and commercial contract law.

## 4.5 The boundary against legal advice

CompliHub360 is **not** a law firm, a tax practice or a regulated adviser, and says so on the site, in the footer and in its own code. Legal, tax and other regulated professionals are an independent provider category inside the marketplace.

The platform states what the law requires and cites the source. It does not apply the law to a specific set of facts, does not render an opinion, does not sign off, and does not accept responsibility for the matter. Its published position, in its own words: the platform maps obligations and surfaces what is likely to apply to an operation, and is not legal advice; when a matter needs a binding opinion, it connects the company to a verified partner who can give one.

## 4.6 What the current draft wording may not reach

- **Know-your-business, beneficial ownership and anti-money-laundering** as a named field.
- **Consumer protection and commercial contract law** as a named field.
- **Delivery channel.** The draft says "via a website". The same information is also delivered as a portable report and through in-account surfaces.
- **Regulatory-change alerts**, which the applicant intends to claim in this class as information rather than in Class 42 as monitoring software, on the reasoning at 3.5.
- **Verification of providers' professional credentials**, which may belong in this class rather than in Class 35.

**Possibly broader than the product:** "environmental protection". The applicant confirms the field as intended, and it is now partly in use — see 3.4 — but the part reaching beyond producer responsibility and packaging remains intention rather than use.

## 4.7 Questions for counsel

Does the personalised obligation report change the information-versus-advice analysis? Should the wording extend beyond "via a website" to cover the portable report and in-account delivery? And should the alerting function be claimed here rather than in Class 42?
